Abstract

Shadow removal in remote sensing imagery is challenged by the limited availability of paired shadow and shadow-free training images, as well as the difficulty of recovering appearance across shadow boundaries. This work combines illumination modeling with generative learning in a framework for shadow synthesis and removal. The generative shadow synthesis network (GSS-Net) integrates a shadow illumination attenuation model with learned style correction to create paired training samples, forming the RS-SynShadow dataset. The generative shadow removal network (GSR-Net) incorporates histogram-enhanced illumination and combines color and structure information to reconstruct shadow-free imagery. Visual results on synthetic and real remote sensing images illustrate the synthesis and removal stages of the framework.

Generative Shadow Synthesis Network (GSS-Net)

GSS-Net combines a shadow illumination attenuation (SIA) model with a style correction generator. The illumination model produces initial synthetic shadows, and the generator refines their appearance using real shadow imagery with a local discriminator. Together with the corresponding shadow-free backgrounds, these synthesized images form the paired RS-SynShadow dataset for training the shadow removal network.

Generative Shadow Removal Network (GSR-Net)

GSR-Net incorporates a histogram-enhanced illumination (HEI) model to obtain a detail-enhanced image from the shadow input. Color and structure encoders extract complementary features, which are combined through adaptive feature fusion and cross-attention fusion. A cascaded decoder reconstructs the shadow-free image, with umbra and penumbra processing providing shadow-region information.
